**Yes. Having student loans does not automatically keep you from buying a house.** What matters is how your student loan payment fits alongside the proposed housing payment, your other debts, your qualifying income, and the loan program’s requirements.

The question I hear is often, “Do I need to pay off my student loans before I can get a mortgage?” Usually, the better first step is to find out **which monthly payment the lender must use**. That figure can be different from what you expect.

## How do student loans affect mortgage approval?

Lenders calculate your [debt-to-income ratio](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b3-6-02/debt-income-ratios), or DTI, by comparing your monthly debt obligations with your gross monthly qualifying income. Your proposed housing payment is part of that calculation, along with debts such as car loans, credit cards, and student loans. The housing figure can include [principal, interest, property taxes, insurance, mortgage insurance, and association dues](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b3-6-03/monthly-housing-expense-subject-property) when applicable.

For a conventional loan following **Fannie Mae’s rules**, the lender may generally use the student loan payment shown on your credit report. If that amount is incorrect, the lender may use the payment on your most recent student loan statement. The rules become more specific when the credit report shows no payment or shows $0.

- **Documented $0 income-driven repayment payment:** Fannie Mae allows a lender to qualify you using $0 when student loan documentation verifies that your actual payment under an income-driven plan is $0.
- **Deferred loan or loan in forbearance:** Fannie Mae allows the lender to use either **1% of the outstanding balance** or a fully amortizing payment calculated from documented repayment terms. A pause in payments does not, by itself, mean the lender can ignore the loan.

Those [Fannie Mae student loan guidelines](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b3-6-05/monthly-debt-obligations) are not a promise that every lender or loan program will calculate your payment the same way. FHA, VA, and other programs have their own requirements, and a lender may have additional rules.

## A simple example

Suppose your gross qualifying income is **$7,000 a month**, your proposed total housing payment is **$2,400**, and your other monthly debts total **$300**. You also have a **$60,000 student loan balance**.

| Student loan payment used | Total monthly obligations | Example DTI |
|---|---:|---:|
| $400 documented payment | $3,100 | 44.3% |
| $600 payment, equal to 1% of the balance | $3,300 | 47.1% |

This example shows why the **qualifying payment**, not just the student loan balance, matters. It does not establish approval: the lender still has to review your full application, credit, assets, property, and applicable underwriting results. Fannie Mae’s published DTI limits also vary by underwriting method and circumstances.

## Should I pay off my student loans before buying?

Maybe, but I would run the numbers first. Paying down a student loan could improve the monthly payment used for qualification in some situations. It could also use cash you need for a down payment, closing costs, or reserves. A lender can compare those options using your actual loan statements and a realistic housing payment.

Qualification and comfort are also different questions. The [Consumer Financial Protection Bureau](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/how-can-i-figure-out-if-i-can-afford-to-buy-a-home-and-take-out-a-mortgage-en-118/) recommends looking at what fits your budget and savings priorities, even if a lender would approve a larger payment.

## Quick questions

**Can I get a mortgage with a $0 student loan payment?**
Possibly. Under Fannie Mae’s conventional guidelines, a documented $0 income-driven repayment payment may be used as $0 for qualification. A deferred loan with no payment is treated differently.

**Does student loan deferment help me qualify?**
Do not assume it will. Under Fannie Mae’s rules, a deferred student loan may require a calculated payment even while you are not making payments.

**What should I send my lender?**
Start with your most recent student loan statements and any documentation showing your current repayment plan and required payment. If the credit report is out of date, those documents can help the lender determine which figure the program permits.
