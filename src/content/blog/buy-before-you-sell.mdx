If you're like many homeowners I talk to every week, you've probably asked yourself one big question:

> **"Should I sell my current home first, or buy my next home before I sell?"**

It's one of the biggest decisions you'll make during the moving process—and unfortunately, there isn't a one-size-fits-all answer.

Some families worry about carrying two mortgage payments.

Others worry they'll sell too early and have nowhere to live.

Many are afraid they'll lose the perfect house because they haven't sold their current home yet.

The good news is there are more options than many homeowners realize.

Let's walk through them.

---

## Why This Decision Feels So Stressful

For most homeowners, their current house represents their largest financial asset.

That creates questions like:

- How much equity do I have?
- Can I access that equity before selling?
- Will I qualify for another mortgage?
- Should my offer be contingent on selling?
- What happens if my current home doesn't sell right away?

The answers depend on your financial picture—not just the market.

---

## Option One: Sell First

Selling first is the traditional path.

### Advantages

- Know exactly how much equity you'll have available.
- Avoid carrying two mortgage payments.
- Simpler qualification process.
- More financial certainty.

### Disadvantages

You may find yourself:

- Renting temporarily
- Living with family
- Paying for storage
- Feeling rushed into buying your next home

---

## Option Two: Buy Before Selling

For many homeowners, buying first creates a much smoother transition.

### Advantages

- Move only once.
- Take your time preparing your current home.
- Avoid temporary housing.
- Reduce moving stress.

### Challenges

Buying before selling isn't right for everyone.

You may need to qualify while still owning your current home, and you'll need a strategy for using your available equity wisely.

---

## Financing Strategies That May Help

Many people assume selling first is their only option.

Often, it isn't.

Depending on your situation, strategies may include:

- Using existing equity
- Bridge-style financing
- Temporary financing solutions
- Programs designed specifically for move-up buyers
- Other mortgage solutions based on your financial goals

The best approach depends entirely on your circumstances.

---

## Four Common Mistakes

### Waiting Until the Home Is Listed

The best time to develop a financing strategy is before your home ever hits the market.

### Shopping Before Getting Pre-Approved

Understanding your buying power first gives you confidence and negotiating strength.

### Assuming There's Only One Option

Many homeowners are surprised to discover financing solutions they didn't know existed.

### Focusing Only on Interest Rate

Sometimes the best mortgage strategy isn't simply the lowest rate—it's the one that helps you achieve your goals with the least amount of stress.

---

## When Buying Before Selling Makes Sense

Buying first is often worth exploring if you're:

- Moving into a larger home
- Relocating for work
- Downsizing
- Moving into a preferred school district
- Purchasing in a competitive market

---

## Planning Makes All the Difference

The homeowners with the smoothest moves usually begin planning months before they list their homes.

That gives them time to:

- Understand their equity
- Review financing options
- Prepare their current home
- Build a realistic timeline
- Strengthen their negotiating position

Planning reduces uncertainty—and uncertainty creates stress.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I buy a home before selling my current home?

In many cases, yes. Your income, equity, debt, and overall financial picture determine which financing strategies may be available.

### Do I have to qualify with two mortgage payments?

Not always. Several financing options may help depending on your individual circumstances.

### What happens if my current home doesn't sell immediately?

Planning ahead allows you to prepare for different scenarios and choose a strategy that reduces unnecessary stress.

### Should I talk with a lender before listing my home?

Absolutely. The earlier you understand your options, the more flexibility you'll typically have during your move.
