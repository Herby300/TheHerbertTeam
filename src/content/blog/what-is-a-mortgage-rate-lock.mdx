When you are choosing a mortgage, the rate is an important part of the conversation. So is the question of how long an offered rate will remain available.

Before deciding when to lock, ask the lender to explain the written terms and how they fit your planned closing. A useful decision starts with understanding the offer in front of you, rather than trying to predict the next headline.

> **Quick answer:** A mortgage rate lock protects an offered interest rate for a specified period, subject to its conditions. Discuss the actual agreement, closing timeline, and costs with your lender before deciding when to lock. This article cannot identify the best market day for your transaction.

## What does a rate lock do?

The CFPB explains that a lock generally keeps the offered rate from changing before closing if you close within the specified period and your application does not change. An unlocked rate can change. The CFPB notes that locks are commonly offered for 30, 45, or 60 days, and sometimes longer, but lock policies vary, so ask about the duration, cost, conditions, and what happens if rates fall. [CFPB: rate locks](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/whats-a-lock-in-or-a-rate-lock-en-143/)

A lock is not a promise that nothing can change. According to the CFPB, a locked rate can still change if, for example, your loan amount, down payment, appraisal, credit score, or verified income changes. Ask the lender which changes would affect your lock. [CFPB: rate locks](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/whats-a-lock-in-or-a-rate-lock-en-143/)

For your own planning, write down what you understand the offer to mean. Then ask the loan officer to confirm or correct your notes. Keep the written information with the rest of your mortgage paperwork.

A short note can be more useful than a vague recollection of a phone conversation: “Here is the option I am considering, here is my planned closing date, and here are the questions I need answered.”

## Does receiving an estimate mean I am locked?

The CFPB says some lenders lock when issuing a Loan Estimate, while others require an expression of intent to proceed first. Check the rate-lock information at the top of page one of the Loan Estimate and confirm the status with the lender. If closing runs past the lock's expiration, an extension may carry a fee, even when the lender's processing time caused the delay. [CFPB: choosing a loan offer](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/owning-a-home/compare/choose-loan-offer/)

Do not rely on the word “quoted” as your confirmation. Ask what action is needed, who confirms completion, and where you will find the written terms. If there is anything you do not understand, resolve it before treating the decision as complete.

## Start with the closing timeline

Put your intended closing date beside the terms you are considering. Ask the mortgage team which milestones remain open and whether the timeframe appears realistic for your file.

You can use a simple discussion sheet:

| Question | What to write down |
| --- | --- |
| What option am I reviewing? | The actual written proposal |
| What is the current closing plan? | The date and unresolved milestones |
| What deadline applies? | The lender's stated expiration details |
| What happens if plans change? | The explanation and any follow-up needed |
| Who should I contact? | The person responsible for the next step |

This is a personal organization tool, not a lock agreement or a complete underwriting checklist.

## A fictional timing example

Jordan is comparing a lender's written lock options while planning a home purchase. The closing date is still being coordinated, and several questions remain unanswered.

Instead of choosing solely from the headline rate, Jordan asks the loan officer to walk through the timeframe, the terms of each option, and what would happen if the date changed.

Jordan keeps a list of open questions and follows up before the next decision. If the closing plan changes, Jordan sends the update to the mortgage team rather than assuming the earlier discussion still answers every question.

Jordan is fictional. No lock period, fee, rate, or transaction result is recommended.

## What if the figures change before closing?

The CFPB advises asking the lender for a specific explanation when the rate or fees differ between the Loan Estimate and Closing Disclosure. Application changes can affect a locked offer, and legal rules govern certain closing-cost changes. A change is a reason to request an explanation, not to assume either that everything is permitted or that an error has occurred. [CFPB: changed rates or fees](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/my-rate-or-the-fees-changed-between-my-loan-estimate-and-my-closing-disclosure-what-do-i-do-en-184/)

Keep the documents you are comparing and identify the item that concerns you. A clear question might be: “This figure differs from the earlier document. Please explain the reason and what I should review.”

This article does not interpret a specific agreement, calculate permitted fee changes, or provide a legal conclusion. Bring unresolved legal questions to an appropriate professional.

## How should I decide when to lock?

Start with questions that can be answered about your own file:

- What written terms are available for the financing I am considering?
- What is known about my closing timeline?
- Which important assumptions remain unverified?
- What costs and conditions should I understand before deciding?
- What follow-up will be needed after I make the decision?

Ask the loan officer to explain the tradeoffs in plain language. Consider how the proposal fits your household plan and the decision you need to make.

A conversation about timing should help you understand your choices. It should not require you to become a market forecaster. No one reading this article should treat it as a direction to lock or float on a particular date.

## Keep the communication simple

If your plans change, tell the mortgage team what changed and when. Ask whether the information affects the proposal you are reviewing and what records are needed.

Use the secure process arranged with the professional handling your file. Do not post personal disclosures, application details, or account information in public comments or social messages.

Keep copies of what you receive and provide. If an explanation is unclear, write it back in your own words and ask whether you understood it correctly.

## Frequently asked questions

### Will this article tell me whether rates will rise tomorrow?
No. It is a preparation guide for discussing an actual offer, not a market forecast or a recommendation to time interest rates.

### If rates fall, will I automatically get a different rate?
Ask the lender what the written policy provides. Do not assume a repricing or float-down option exists for your offer or that it has no conditions or costs.

### What if my planned closing date changes?
Contact the mortgage team promptly. Ask how the new timeline relates to the written terms and which next steps need attention.

### Does a rate lock mean my mortgage is approved?
Do not use a pricing discussion as proof of approval. Ask the mortgage team what stage of review your application has reached and what remains outstanding.

### What is one useful thing I can do today?
Make a list of your timeline questions and ask the lender to explain the actual written options. Keep the answers with your mortgage documents.
